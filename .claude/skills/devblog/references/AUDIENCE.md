# AUDIENCE.md — who reads the devblog, and the promise every post keeps

## The reader

Smart, curious, and not an engineer. A designer, an artist, a producer, a friend from another field. They have
heard of AI coding tools and have opinions; they have not used one for a week straight. They do not know what a
G-buffer is and do not need to. They will read 4,000 words if the first 200 earn it.

Assume they:

- read for the story of a person making something, and stay for the how;
- can follow one technical idea at a time if it is explained in the sentence that introduces it;
- distrust hype about AI, and are right to;
- remember a number when it is attached to a moment ("the estimate was 14 milliseconds; the measurement was 2").

## The promise every post keeps

> *Here is what I did differently. Here is how it worked out, or failed. Here is why you should try it.*

Every post makes all three moves. Not hedged ("this might work for you"), not sold ("this will change how you
work"). Stated, with the evidence beside it.

## Terms

Every term from SpriteJammer's glossary is explained the first time it appears, in the sentence itself or the one
after, or it is not used. The explanation is for the reader above, not for an engineer. When a post explains a
term, the run's ledger entry lists it under *Terms explained*, so the next post can use it with a link instead of
an explanation.

Words that need explaining the first time, every time: agent, tick, frame, benchmark, spike, ADR, journal (as
this project uses it), gate, worktree, pull request, CI, milestone, the difference between an estimate and a
measurement. Words that never appear without a plain-language gloss: G-buffer, deferred, impostor, Numba,
numpy, SoA, LOD, wgpu, HDR.

## What the reader must never have to do

- Look something up to follow the paragraph.
- Wonder whether a number was measured or guessed.
- Guess who "we" is. There is one person and a set of tools; say which did what.
- Trust the writer's enthusiasm in place of evidence.
