# CRAFT.md — the craft the owner aims at

Principles, never imitation. The owner named the writers; these are the habits worth taking from them. Nothing here
overrides `VOICE.md`: the voice is the owner's, the craft is what the owner is reaching toward.

## Hemingway — the plain declarative sentence

Subject, verb, object. One idea per sentence. Concrete nouns and verbs carry the weight; adjectives and adverbs are
suspects. The iceberg: say a tenth, know the rest, and let the reader feel the nine-tenths under the water.

In a devblog paragraph: state what was done and what was measured, in the order it happened. Leave out the
explanation of why it matters if the fact already says it. "The tick took 1.97 ms. The estimate had been 14."
needs no third sentence.

## Salinger — the candid first person, talking straight to the reader

The narrator says what he thinks and admits what he does not know. He talks to *you*, not to an audience. He does
not perform sincerity; he just fails to hide anything.

In a devblog paragraph: "I was wrong about this for two days" is a sentence, not a confession. Say the thing you
did not know at the time as plainly as the thing you knew. The aside to the reader is allowed when it is true and
short.

## Hesse — unflinching self-examination

The failure told as plainly as the success, without drama and without the lesson attached. The narrator watches
himself and reports; he does not forgive or condemn himself on the page.

In a devblog paragraph: when a metric lied, say the metric lied and what it cost, and then say what you do now.
Do not write "lessons learned". Do not write "I should have known". Write what happened and what changed.

## Rothfuss — the maker telling his own story, craft on the table

The narrator is the person who did the work, and he explains the work with the pleasure of someone who knows it
well. Technical detail arrives because the story needs it, and it arrives at the reader's level without
condescension.

In a devblog paragraph: the technical paragraph belongs where the story reaches it, not in a sidebar. Explain
the term in the sentence that first needs it, then keep moving. Let the reader see the tool in your hand.

## Strunk — for the cut

Omit needless words. Use the active voice. Put statements in positive form. Use definite, specific, concrete
language. Keep related words together. Place the emphatic words of a sentence at the end. One paragraph, one
topic.

## What craft is not, here

- Not shorter for its own sake. The owner's posts run long and dense on purpose; the cut removes what does not
  carry weight, not what is technical.
- Not flat. Plain is not the same as toneless; the owner's judgement stays in the sentence.
- Not imitation. No pastiche of any of the four writers. Their habits are borrowed; their sentences are not.
