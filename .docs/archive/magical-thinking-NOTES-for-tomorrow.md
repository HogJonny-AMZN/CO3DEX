# Notes for tomorrow — ideas to work into "Without Magical Thinking"

Three late-night ideas from Jonny (2026-07-19), captured so we don't lose them.

## 1. Appeal to antiquity ("it's old," "this is the way," "stands the test of time")
- A named magical-thinking move: **age offered as evidence of truth.**
- Rebuttal built in: persistence isn't proof. Bloodletting was ancient. Surviving beliefs are good at *surviving* (sticky, comforting, useful for control), not necessarily true.
- **Resolves a tension in our own opener:** the essay valorizes old ("story is the spine of human existence across eons, older than writing"). The distinction that saves it: antiquity makes a *practice* venerable (storytelling is an old, honest human thing); antiquity is NOT evidence for a *claim*. Religion conflates "our story is ancient" (true) with "our claims are true *because* ancient" (fallacy). Same honest/broken contract: the age of the telling doesn't make the tale factual.
- Also pre-empts the "but you praised old stories!" objection.
- **Placement options:** deepen the opener, or a short beat near the primer/obedience intro (age = another false credential), or fold into "Who fact-checks the prophet."

## 2. The literalism spectrum
- Verbatim-inerrancy reader (Bible as physics) = full magical thinking.
- Parable reader ("story, not physics, but still true") = closer to the honest contract; they know it's a story.
- The slippery part: what "still true" means — honest moral/narrative truth, OR a **motte-and-bailey** (retreat to "it's just metaphor" when challenged, then live/vote as if literal).
- **Placement:** a steelman/nuance beat near the intro or the honest-contract discussion; sharpens who the target actually is.

## 3. "Backpocket theology" — Jonny's coinage, about his dad (the gem)
- A circular, self-supporting belief system that *feels* rigorous because church gave it the texture of rigor (catechism, apologetics, confident answers) — but no real history, objectivity, evidence, or reckoning with uncomfortable data.
- It's the inherited, in-the-family version of two things already in the essay: **"apologetics is the tell"** and **"faith wearing a lab coat."** Backpocket theology = a lab coat handed to you as a kid that you never took off.
- **This is the fix for the essay's biggest weakness (emotional distance / score note #3).** A short, loving, NOT-a-hit-piece beat about his dad would give the piece a human pulse.
- **Placement:** a personal vignette, likely near the Milk Before Meat / apologetics material or the Bishop/authority section.
