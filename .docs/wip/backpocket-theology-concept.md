# Concept Memo: Backpocket Theology

**Status**: WIP / Future Post Concept  
**Working Title**: *Backpocket Theology: The Closed Loop of Inherited Rigor* (or *The Lab Coat You Never Took Off*)  
**Category Target**: `life` / `philosophy`  
**Author**: Jonathan Galloway  

---

## 1. Core Definition & Thesis

**Backpocket Theology**: A pre-assembled, self-supporting framework of beliefs, catechisms, and apologetics that an individual carries around everywhere like a wallet.

- **The Texture of Rigor without the Friction**: It *feels* like deep, serious intellectual work because it has the appearance and vocabulary of scholarship (scripture cross-references, historical-sounding harmonization, systematic answers for every objection).
- **The Closed Room**: It is an intellectual loop running in isolation. It only works inside its own hermeneutic circle. The moment it encounters real external evidence, primary-source history, or empirical falsification, the loop short-circuits.
- **The Inheritance**: It wasn't researched or chosen; it was handed down in childhood like an heirloom suit, tailored to fit before the wearer had developed the tools to evaluate the stitching.

## 2. Key Themes & Personal Beats to Explore

### A. The Dad Vignette (Loving, Grounded, Empathetic)

- Frame this with genuine affection and respect, not mockery.
- Explore what it's like to watch a good, intelligent, hardworking father navigate the world with a ready-made doctrinal answer for every crisis, doubt, and life transition.
- The distinction between being **intellectually dishonest** and being **cognitively insulated**: believers aren't consciously lying; they genuinely believe their backpocket notes are an objective map of reality.

### B. The Anatomy of the Toolkit

What lives in the "backpocket"?

1. **The Thought-Terminating Cliché**: Quick phrases that shut down ambiguity (*"God works in mysterious ways,"* *"Doubt your doubts before you doubt your faith,"* *"Milk before meat"*).
2. **Harmonization Machinery**: Complex rationalizations that explain away glaring contradictions (e.g., Book of Abraham anachronisms, changing church policies, failed prophecies) by moving goalposts.
3. **The Preemptive Inoculation**: Warnings given in advance about outsiders, critics, and "anti-Mormon" sources to ensure external data is discarded without inspection.

### C. The Cost of Wearing the Lab Coat

- When you are handed a comprehensive answer key at age 8, you never learn how to sit in the discomfort of *"I don't know."*
- **The Crisis of First Contact**: What happens when life delivers a shock that the backpocket cards can't solve (grief, trauma, institutional betrayal, discovery of church history)?
- The painful transition from having an answer for everything to realizing the answer key was printed by the organization that sold you the questions.

### D. Modern Secular Parallels: Backpocket Tech & AI Dogma

- How backpocket thinking manifests outside church walls:
  - **Crypto & Web3 Maxis**: Pre-packaged ideological talking points defending speculative bubbles.
  - **AI Technotheology / AGI Accelerators**: Repeating Silicon Valley eschatology verbatim (*"exponential compute,"* *"inevitable digital consciousness"*).
  - **Corporate Culture**: Inherited business jargon and management dogma treated as fundamental truth.

### E. The Personal Mirror: The Bricoleur Who Pointed the Debugger at the Shelter

- **The Universal Starting Line**: Every human child begins as a *bricoleur*. Under uncertainty and chaos, the young brain grabs whatever scrap wood, catechisms, and moral scaffolding are on the floor of their forest to build a shelter for survival, safety, and belonging. It isn't a defect; it's how children make the world coherent.
- **Escapism as the Game (Bridge to Terabithia)**: Loving books like *Bridge to Terabithia* growing up—you cross the creek, enter the woods, and build an imaginary kingdom out of fallen branches and raw imagination.
  - *Keep the metaphors clean*: Nature is nature. Home is human construction. Escapism is building a world in the trees with rules you invented.
  - The honest contract of childhood play: Terabithia is real in your chest and heart, but you know the creek is wet and the rope swing is just rope. You don't demand the town worship the treehouse.
- **The Divergence (The Story Hook)**: Most people spend their entire lives inside that original cobbled shelter, nailing new apologetic planks over leaks whenever reality seeps in.
- **The Collision with the Day Job**: 30 years inside AAA game engines and technical art meant spending 40+ hours a week in a strict empirical loop:
  - Deconstructing false realities down to vertices and assembly instructions.
  - Asking why the shader is lying or why the frame dropped.
  - Refusing to call a broken pipeline "sacred."
- **The Reckoning**: The turning point where you take the exact same debugger used on 3D engines and point it backward at your own childhood shelter—auditing the nails, testing the foundation against external data, taking off the inherited lab coat, and stepping into the raw, honest open air of *"I don't know."*

## 3. Potential Outlines / Narrative Arcs

1. **Introduction**: The moment you realize your parents' certainty isn't universal truth, but a specific, localized toolkit.
2. **Terabithia in the Trees**: Childhood escapism, building castles from fallen branches on the forest floor, and the clean contract of imagination.
3. **The Anatomy of the Closed Loop**: How backpocket theology mimics intellectual rigor while strictly forbidding external falsification.
4. **A Tribute to the People Who Carry It**: Why good, smart people stay inside the loop (community, psychological safety, identity, filial loyalty).
5. **The Debugger in the Living Room**: The story of taking tools built for deconstructing game engines and turning them on the inherited family theology.
6. **The Unraveling & The Stepping Out**: Taking off the childhood lab coat; the terror and relief of losing the pre-printed answer key.
7. **The Alternative**: Intellectual humility, empirical curiosity, and the courage to say *"I don't know"* in public.

## Appendix: Scientific & Academic Research Foundations

The concept of "backpocket theology" aligns directly with several established bodies of empirical research across cognitive science, sociology of religion, linguistics, and neuroscience:

### 1. Folk Religion & "Theological Incorrectness" (Sociology of Religion)

- **Justin L. Barrett** (*Mind and Religion*, 2005): Coined **"Theological Incorrectness"**—finding that even devout, doctrinally trained believers unconsciously revert to intuitive, anthropomorphic mental shortcuts when reasoning through daily challenges.
- **Meredith McGuire** (*Lived Religion: Faith and Practice in Everyday Life*, 2008): Demonstrated that individuals build a personal **"bricolage"**—a customized mosaic of childhood lessons, folk adages, and institutional habits cobbled together to keep life emotionally and morally coherent.

### 2. The Illusion of Explanatory Depth & Identity-Protective Cognition (Cognitive Science)

- **Leonid Rozenblit & Frank Keil** (2002, *Cognitive Science*): Identified the **Illusion of Explanatory Depth (IOED)**—people mistake familiarity with terminology and catechisms for true causal, mechanistic understanding.
- **Dan Kahan** (Yale Cultural Cognition Project): Documented **Identity-Protective Cognition**—showing that intelligence and cognitive skill are often deployed not to seek objective truth, but to rationalize and protect beliefs that maintain standing within one's community.

### 3. Thought-Terminating Clichés & Attitude Inoculation (Linguistics & Social Psychology)

- **Robert Jay Lifton** (*Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism*, 1961): Identified **thought-terminating clichés**—memorized, high-order generalizations designed to halt critical thought and cognitive dissonance on command (*"Doubt your doubts,"* *"Milk before meat"*).
- **William J. McGuire** (1964, *Attitude Inoculation Theory*): Demonstrated how institutions pre-expose members to weakened, pre-digested criticisms paired with ready-made rebuttals to inoculate them against real counter-evidence.

### 4. Epistemic Bubbles vs. Echo Chambers (Social Epistemology)

- **C. Thi Nguyen** (*Epistemic Bubbles and Echo Chambers*, 2020, *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research*): Analyzed how **echo chambers** systematically discredit all outside sources in advance, turning contrary evidence into proof of external malice or deception.

### 5. Neurobiology of Certainty & Cognitive Closure (Neuroscience)

- **Arie Kruglanski** (1996, *Psychological Review*): Defined the **Need for Cognitive Closure (NFC)**—the psychological drive to eliminate ambiguity and "freeze" on settled answers.
- **Michael Inzlicht et al.** (2009, *Psychological Science*): Used EEG to measure the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC, the brain's conflict/error alarm) and found that strong conviction actively dampens neural distress responses to contradiction, providing a neurological buffer against cognitive dissonance.
